# Introduction

{{dropcap W}}elcome to Lordaeron. The kingdom has fallen, but the war for it is far from over.

*The Scarlet Crusade* is a campaign for **Dragonbane**, written for use with **Bane of Azeroth**. The player characters are newly trained members of the Scarlet Crusade, stationed in Hearthglen in the Western Plaguelands. They already know the routines of garrison life, their immediate superiors, and the walls they are expected to defend. What they have not yet experienced is the war in earnest.

The campaign follows their service from those first assignments through a growing conflict that will take them beyond Hearthglen and deeper into the ruined lands of Lordaeron. Along the way they will fight the Scourge, investigate the Cult of the Damned, travel between Scarlet strongholds, and gradually learn that not every threat to the Crusade comes from outside its ranks.

The campaign is divided into five acts. Each act marks a change in the player characters' understanding of the war, the Scarlet Crusade, and their own place within it. Their earliest duties should feel necessary, ordinary, and often worthwhile, giving them time to know Hearthglen, trust their comrades, and understand why the Crusade exists before that certainty is challenged.

### Using This Book

To play this campaign you need the **Dragonbane** core rules and the **Bane of Azeroth** supplement.

Rules, spells, weapons, and Heroic Abilities from those books may be referenced without being reproduced here. NPCs and creatures required to prepare and run the campaign are instead presented with the game statistics needed where they appear.

Some optional material makes use of rules from the **Book of Magic**. Such material is clearly marked and can be ignored without affecting the main campaign.

This book is written for the Gamemaster rather than the players. It describes the history and current state of Lordaeron, the forces acting within it, and the structure of the campaign before presenting the five acts themselves.

The first of these begins in Hearthglen, where the characters' training has ended and their real service is about to begin.

## History

The Scarlet Crusade was not born from madness or blind fanaticism. It grew out of the ruins of Lordaeron, from soldiers, paladins, and survivors who had already seen their kingdom destroyed by plague, betrayal, and the undead. To understand what the Crusade is at the beginning of this campaign, it is necessary to understand what came before it.

### The Order of the Silver Hand

The Knights of the Silver Hand were founded by Archbishop Alonsus Faol before the Second War. Its paladins combined martial skill with devotion to the Holy Light, and men such as Uther the Lightbringer became celebrated defenders of Lordaeron. Long after the Horde had been defeated, the order remained one of humanity's foremost bulwarks against supernatural threats.

Among those who served the order were many of the men who would later shape the resistance in Lordaeron: Saidan Dathrohan, Alexandros Mograine, Isillien, Abbendis and others. When rumors of plague and strange death cults began to spread through the northern provinces, the Silver Hand once again took up arms in defense of the kingdom.

### The Fall of Lordaeron

The threat was unlike anything the order had faced before. The necromancer Kel'Thuzad and his Cult of the Damned had contaminated grain stored at Andorhal with the Plague of Undeath. From one of the principal grain-distribution centers of northern Lordaeron, the infected shipments spread outward into surrounding settlements. Those who succumbed to the plague did not simply die, they rose again as servants of the Scourge.

Prince Arthas Menethil and Jaina Proudmoore traced the plague through Andorhal and Hearthglen. At Hearthglen, Arthas witnessed its effects firsthand, and the pursuit eventually led him toward Stratholme. There he discovered that infected grain had already reached the population. Convinced that the city would soon become an army of undead, Arthas ordered its inhabitants killed before they could turn. Uther refused the command, and Arthas suspended the paladins of the Silver Hand before carrying out the culling without them.

Arthas pursued the dreadlord Mal'Ganis to Northrend. When he eventually returned to Lordaeron, it was not as its prince but as a death knight in the service of the Lich King. He murdered his father, King Terenas, and led the Scourge against the kingdom he had once sworn to defend. Lordaeron collapsed, Uther was slain, and the surviving defenders of the realm were scattered across a land increasingly dominated by the dead.

The fertile Eastweald became what is now known as the Plaguelands. Andorhal lay ruined, Stratholme became a stronghold of the Scourge, and countless smaller settlements were destroyed or abandoned. Yet pockets of human resistance survived.

### The Resistance

Survivors of the Silver Hand regrouped rather than abandon Lordaeron. The town of Hearthglen became one of the places where the remaining paladins and soldiers gathered, with Taelan Fordring offering his family's lands as a base of operations. Their purpose was straightforward: defend those who remained and reclaim Lordaeron from the undead.

Not all of them agreed on what the resistance should become. Some argued for accepting allies from beyond Lordaeron and beyond humanity, while others increasingly saw the salvation of the fallen kingdom as a human struggle. The horrors of the Scourge, the loss of their homeland, the years of fighting an enemy capable of hiding among the living all hardened the survivors. The seeds of the divisions that would later define Lordaeron's resistance were already present.

Among their greatest champions was Highlord Alexandros Mograine, wielder of the Ashbringer, a weapon forged to destroy the undead. Mograine became one of the most formidable enemies of the Scourge and a symbol around whom the surviving paladins could rally.

But another enemy had already found its way into their ranks.

### Dathrohan and Balnazzar

During the fighting around Stratholme, the paladin Saidan Dathrohan was killed. His body was taken by **Balnazzar**, a nathrezim dreadlord who thereafter wore Dathrohan's identity and worked from within the surviving forces of the Silver Hand. To Dathrohan's comrades, their leader had returned from battle. They had no reason to know that the man standing before them was a demon.

Balnazzar had reason to oppose the undead. The Scourge had broken from the control of the Burning Legion, and the dreadlord could turn Lordaeron's surviving warriors against enemies that were also his own. But an independent and united resistance led by men such as Alexandros Mograine would be much harder to control.

{{pageNumber,auto Introduction}}
\page

Unable to bend Mograine to his will, Balnazzar instead manipulated his son Renault. Renault led his father and High Inquisitor Fairbanks into an ambush near Stratholme. After a long battle against the undead, the exhausted Alexandros dropped the Ashbringer, and Renault took up the sword and murdered his father with it. The betrayal corrupted both the weapon and the legacy surrounding it, while the Scourge later claimed Alexandros' body.

With Mograine gone, Balnazzar's influence over the remaining resistance grew. Wearing Dathrohan's face, he helped reshape the remnants of the Silver Hand in Lordaeron into the **Scarlet Crusade**, an order devoted to the destruction of the undead. Those who rejected its growing extremism and distrusted its leadership broke away, contributing to the formation of the Argent Dawn.

The Scarlet Crusade endured because the war it fought was real. The Scourge still occupied much of Lordaeron, the Cult of the Damned remained active, and the surviving human settlements genuinely depended upon armed defenders. Its soldiers reclaimed fortifications, protected survivors and continued a war that much of the world had already lost.

What almost none of them knew was that the man directing that war from the highest levels of their order was one of the very demons they had sworn to destroy.

## Current Situation

Lordaeron is no longer a kingdom, but neither is it an empty wasteland. Human strongholds endure among the ruins, the Scourge still controls great stretches of the countryside, and several rival powers fight over what remains. Roads that were once used by merchants and farmers now connect isolated fortifications, abandoned settlements and battlefields where the fighting has never truly ended.

The Scarlet Crusade is one of the strongest surviving human forces in the region. To its members, the war is far from over. The undead still occupy their homeland, the Cult of the Damned remains active, and every reclaimed farm, road and village is proof that Lordaeron has not yet been lost.

### The Scarlet Crusade

The Scarlet Crusade is a militant order dedicated to the destruction of the undead and the reclamation of Lordaeron. It maintains fortified positions across the Plaguelands, including Hearthglen in the north, the Scarlet Monastery in the west, Tyr's Hand in the east, and a presence within Stratholme itself. Hearthglen in particular serves as one of the Crusade's important bases in the Western Plaguelands, from which its soldiers strike nearby Scourge forces.

The Crusade is disciplined, heavily militarized and increasingly suspicious of outsiders. This suspicion is not without cause. Its members fight enemies that can raise the dead, corrupt the living and hide agents among ordinary people. Years of plague, infiltration and loss have taught many crusaders to regard caution as a matter of survival.

At the beginning of this campaign, the Scarlet Crusade should not be presented merely as a band of murderous fanatics. Its soldiers guard roads, escort supplies, hunt undead and protect surviving communities. Many of its members sincerely believe that they are the last defenders of a fallen kingdom.

That belief is one of the Crusade's greatest strengths, but also a weakness for Balnazzar to exploit.

### The Scourge

The Scourge remains the most immediate threat throughout the Plaguelands. Undead infest the ruins of towns and farmsteads, wander the roads and gather around plague cauldrons and other strongholds. In the Western Plaguelands, Andorhal remains one of their major centers, while the surrounding countryside is blighted and populated by undead and diseased wildlife.


For most Scarlet soldiers, this is the enemy they understand best. The Scourge destroyed their homeland, continues to occupy it, and gives them a clear reason to fight.

### The Cult of the Damned

The Cult of the Damned survives alongside the Scourge as its living hand. Its members include necromancers, plague-bearers, infiltrators and ordinary people who have given themselves willingly to the service of undeath.

The Cult's presence is particularly important to the atmosphere of the campaign. A ghoul can usually be recognized for what it is. A cultist cannot.

The Cult has long been associated with the corruption of Lordaeron and with Scholomance, the School of Necromancy beneath Caer Darrow. Even after the kingdom's fall, cultists continue to spread plague, create undead and support Scourge operations.

This means that Scarlet suspicion is sometimes justified. There really are traitors, infiltrators and plague-bearers among the living. The danger lies not in recognizing that fact, but in what the Crusade gradually becomes willing to do because of it.

### The Forsaken

Not all undead in Lordaeron serve the Lich King. The Forsaken, undead who broke free from the Scourge, have established their own power in the ruins of northern Lordaeron. Their existence complicates the war considerably, as to the Scarlet Crusade, the distinction bet  ween a free-willed undead and a servant of the Scourge is often meaningless.

The Forsaken therefore regard the Scarlet Crusade as an enemy, while the Crusade sees them as another manifestation of the same corruption that destroyed Lordaeron.

This conflict becomes increasingly important as the campaign progresses. The Forsaken know things about the undead, the dreadlords and the recent history of Lordaeron that the Crusade either does not know or refuses to believe.

### The Burning Legion

The Burning Legion is not openly waging war across the Plaguelands at the beginning of the campaign, but its influence has not disappeared. The Scourge was originally created as a weapon of the Legion, but the Lich King ultimately broke from his masters. That split left the dreadlords with good reason to oppose the Scourge even after the Legion's larger invasion failed.

Balnazzar's control of the Scarlet Crusade must be understood in that context. He does not need the Crusade to fail militarily. Quite the opposite: Scarlet soldiers who kill Scourge serve his purposes well. What he cannot allow is for the Crusade to become an independent, stable force capable of rebuilding Lordaeron on its own terms.

### The Plaguelands

The campaign is centered on the ruined northern lands of Lordaeron, particularly the Western and Eastern Plaguelands and the territories between them.

#### Hearthglen
is the characters' first home and the principal base for Act I. It is a fortified settlement in the northern Western Plaguelands, centered on Mardenholde Keep and surrounded by roads, woods and working facilities that distinguish it sharply from much of the ruined countryside.

#### Andorhal
lies to the south and remains one of the most important Scourge strongholds in the Western Plaguelands. The town and the surrounding farms still bear the scars of the original plague outbreak.

#### Scholomance
lies under Caer Darrow, further south, where the Cult of the Damned and necromantic learning have deep roots. It will become increasingly important as the characters begin to look beyond ordinary battlefield threats.

#### The Scarlet Monastery
lies west of the Plaguelands and is one of the Crusade's great institutional centers. It represents something different from Hearthglen: doctrine, hierarchy, training and the organization behind the soldiers in the field.

#### Stratholme
lies to the east. Once one of Lordaeron's great cities, it is now a devastated battleground and Scourge stronghold. The Scarlet Crusade still maintains the Scarlet Bastion within the ruined city, making Stratholme both a symbol of what was lost and one of the most dangerous places in the campaign.
<br/><br/>
Beyond these places lie other powers and strongholds, including Tyr's Hand, the Argent Dawn and the Forsaken territories to the west. They form part of the wider conflict even when the characters do not visit them directly.

At the beginning of the campaign, however, the characters' world is much smaller. Their world is Hearthglen. The Scourge is outside the walls, their comrades stand beside them, and the Scarlet Crusade appears to be one of the few forces still willing and able to fight for what remains of Lordaeron.

## Course of Events

The campaign is divided into five acts. Each act represents a shift in how the characters understand the Scarlet Crusade, the war against the Scourge, and their own place within it.

The characters begin as newly trained members of the Crusade stationed in Hearthglen. They have completed their basic training and know the routines of garrison life, but they have not yet been tested in earnest. At first, their assignments are straightforward: patrol roads, protect civilians, investigate missing soldiers, escort supplies, and fight the undead.

Over time, the missions become more difficult to judge. Orders remain militarily plausible, enemies remain real, and harsh decisions often have understandable reasons behind them. What changes is the pattern. The Crusade becomes more isolated, more suspicious, and increasingly willing to sacrifice people and principles in the name of victory.

The characters are not expected to uncover a conspiracy from the beginning. The campaign works best if they first come to trust the Crusade, then begin to notice contradictions, and only later realize that something is wrong at the highest levels of command.

### Running the Campaign

The adventures in this campaign are the important moments in the characters' service, not a complete account of every day they spend in the Scarlet Crusade.

Weeks may pass between major assignments. During that time the characters perform ordinary duties, train, recover, maintain equipment, study magic, work on personal projects, and spend time with the people around them. Most of this should be handled narratively unless something interesting happens.

The campaign tracks time in weeks behind the scenes. This gives the Gamemaster a simple way to handle training, equipment orders, long-term projects, patrol duty, healing, and the gradual change of seasons.

The suggested time between major adventures is given in each act. The Gamemaster may shorten or extend these intervals by roughly a week when needed, but the characters should not know when the next important assignment will arrive. They experience time through duty rosters, changing weather, deliveries, training opportunities, and new orders rather than through a visible campaign schedule.

Routine patrols are an important part of Scarlet service. Each act includes a patrol table appropriate to the current state of the region. Some patrols are mandatory, while opportunities for additional paid patrol duty may be offered during downtime. Ordinary guard duty, drills, escorts, and other routine assignments can usually be summarized without a roll.

The patrol tables become increasingly dangerous as the campaign progresses. In the beginning, most patrols pass without incident. Later, the characters encounter more Scourge activity, greater unrest, and eventually signs that the Crusade itself is beginning to fracture.

At the end of each act, each character is rewarded an additional Heroic Ability of their choice.

### The Truth Behind the Crusade

The Scarlet Crusade does not begin this campaign as a hollow organization waiting to collapse. Its soldiers fight real enemies, defend real communities, and often make sensible decisions in terrible circumstances. This is what makes Balnazzar's influence dangerous.

Wearing the identity of Saidan Dathrohan, Balnazzar does not need the Crusade to lose its battles. He benefits when Scarlet soldiers destroy Scourge forces, Cult of the Damned cells, and other enemies of the Burning Legion. What he must prevent is something more dangerous: a strong, independent, stable human power rising from the ruins of Lordaeron.

Balnazzar encourages actions that make sense in isolation but damage the Crusade in the long term. He rewards obedience over judgment, suspicion over trust, isolation over cooperation, and victories that leave the organization more divided and dependent than before. A village may genuinely contain cultists. A quarantine may genuinely stop plague. A deserter may genuinely be dangerous. A Forsaken agent may genuinely be an enemy. The manipulation lies not in inventing false threats, but in shaping how the Crusade responds to real ones.

As the campaign progresses, the characters should begin to notice a recurring pattern: **Every operation seems to make military sense, yet somehow the Scarlet Crusade is always worse off afterward.**

The characters do not need to discover every detail of Balnazzar's plans to defeat him. They do not need to understand the full history of the Burning Legion, the Lich King, or the dreadlords. They only need to reach the conclusion that whoever is giving these orders must be stopped.

### The Acts

#### Act I --- New Blood

*We belong here.*

The characters begin their active service in Hearthglen. Their first assignments are simple and necessary: protect workers, search for missing soldiers, escort supplies, and investigate threats near Scourge territory. Their officers are generally competent. Their comrades are worth protecting. The dangers they face are real.

Act I should establish Hearthglen as a home rather than merely a quest hub. Give the characters time to train, patrol, form relationships, and become part of the garrison. By the end of the act, they should no longer feel like new recruits. They are known, useful, and trusted. The Scarlet Crusade has given them every reason to believe they chose the right side.

#### Act II --- Necessary Measures

*I hated doing that. But what was the alternative?*

The war becomes harsher. The characters begin receiving assignments where the correct decision is less obvious. Quarantines, deserters, suspected collaborators, and increasingly severe military measures force them to balance compassion against survival.

The Crusade should still appear rational. The important thing is that its harshest decisions often work. This is the act in which the characters begin learning how easy it is to justify terrible actions when the alternative appears worse.

#### Act III --- Cracks in the Faith

*Something is wrong.*

Individual incidents begin to form a pattern. The characters encounter contradictory orders, information that does not fit what they have been told, and people whom the Crusade considers enemies but who know things the characters need to hear.

The war against the Scourge remains real, but it becomes increasingly difficult to explain why Scarlet victories so often leave the organization weaker, more isolated, or more divided. By the end of the act, doubt should no longer be a private discomfort. There is a problem within the Crusade.

#### Act IV --- Enemy Within

*The problem is inside our own command.*

The characters begin acting on what they have learned. Alliances become possible with people the Crusade once taught them to distrust. Officers and soldiers must choose between loyalty to the organization, loyalty to their commanders, and loyalty to what they believe the Crusade was meant to be.

The conflict is no longer simply Scarlet Crusade against its enemies. The Crusade itself begins to fracture. The characters may not yet know exactly what Saidan Dathrohan is, but by the end of this act they should understand that his leadership is at the heart of the problem.

#### Act V --- Stratholme

*Everything has led here.*

The campaign returns to one of the places where Lordaeron's fall began. The characters travel to Stratholme and confront the consequences of everything that has come before: the Scourge, the Scarlet Crusade, the decisions they have made, and the people who have survived alongside them.

The final conflict is not about whether the Scourge is evil or whether the Scarlet Crusade was ever worth fighting for. It is about whether the Crusade can remain what its soldiers believed it was meant to be while Balnazzar still controls it. The characters' relationships, earlier decisions, and the allies they have made should all matter here.

## Player Characters

The Scarlet Crusade is not an adventuring company that accepts anyone willing to fight the undead. It is a military and religious order born from the remnants of Lordaeron and the Order of the Silver Hand, and its membership reflects that history.

The recommendations below are based on the Scarlet Crusade as it is portrayed in Warcraft lore and on the assumptions of this campaign. They are not additional rules for Bane of Azeroth. The Gamemaster always has the final say on which kin, classes, and character concepts are available.

Unusual characters can work, and may create interesting stories, but the campaign assumes that the characters can function as recognized members of the Scarlet Crusade without every scene becoming a question of why they have not been expelled, imprisoned, or killed.

These recommendations are intended to preserve the tone and historical grounding of the campaign, not to restrict how any particular group chooses to play. The Gamemaster is always free to make exceptions or alter the setting assumptions to suit the table. Also note that the recommendations concern kin and the classes introduced in Bane of Azeroth. Professions are not restricted by this section unless the Gamemaster decides otherwise.

### Kin

**Humans** are by far the most natural choice. The Scarlet Crusade is overwhelmingly human and grew out of the surviving forces of Lordaeron and the Order of the Silver Hand.

**High elves** and **dwarves** are possible, but unusual. Both peoples had ties to the Alliance of Lordaeron and to the forces from which the Scarlet Crusade eventually emerged. For game purposes, a high elf can use the Blood Elf kin from Bane of Azeroth.

A high elf or dwarf character should have a strong personal connection to the Crusade or to its predecessors. A fallen relative who served in the Silver Hand, an old family bond to one of its members, or a history of fighting alongside Lordaeron's defenders are all suitable explanations.

Even with such a background, acceptance should not be universal. Some Scarlet soldiers may respect the character's service or family history, while others remain suspicious simply because they are not human. The player should be comfortable with that tension being part of the character's story.

Other kin are not recommended for this campaign. A Gamemaster who wishes to allow one should first consider why the Scarlet Crusade would accept that individual and whether doing so would undermine the campaign's premise.

### Classes

Bane of Azeroth introduces the concept of classes. While it is recommended that those are used in this campaign, some fit naturally into the Scarlet Crusade, while others conflict with either its history, its worldview, or the Warcraft setting of this campaign.

#### Hunters, Mages, Monks, Paladins, Priests, Rogues,
<div style='margin-top:-8px;'></div>

#### and Warriors
all have clear places within the Scarlet Crusade. Paladins and priests reflect the order's roots in the Silver Hand and the Church of the Holy Light. Warriors form the backbone of its military forces, while hunters, scouts, marksmen, rogues, and similar specialists fill obvious practical roles. The Crusade also makes use of mages and arcanists, and martial monks are found among its ranks. Characters of these classes generally require no special explanation beyond why they chose to join the Crusade.

The remaining Bane of Azeroth classes are not recommended for player characters in this campaign for various reasons.

#### Druids and Shamans
are poor fits primarily because they do not have a natural place among the human, high elven, or dwarven traditions represented within the Scarlet Crusade at this point in Azeroth's history. Allowing one would therefore require the Gamemaster to deliberately depart from the normal assumptions of the setting rather than merely explain an unusual individual.

#### Death Knights
are fundamentally incompatible with the Crusade's purpose. They are undead warriors associated with the very powers the Scarlet Crusade exists to destroy.

#### Demon Hunters and Warlocks
openly make use of demonic or fel powers. Even where such individuals might fight enemies of the Scourge, their methods and associations would make acceptance within the Scarlet Crusade extremely unlikely.

#### Evokers
belong to a people and tradition entirely outside the historical foundations of the Crusade and are not a natural fit for the period or premise of this campaign.

# New Blood
{title="Act 1"}

{{dropcap H}}earthglen is a hard place, but not a hopeless one. Its walls stand. Its fires burn. Patrols still return through the gates, wagons still carry timber from the mills, and children still run between buildings that survived the fall of Lordaeron.

For the player characters, this is where the Scarlet Crusade becomes something more than an oath, a uniform, or a distant cause. It becomes people they know, duties they understand, and a place they have helped keep alive.

Act I is about **belonging**.

The player characters begin as new recruits. They are useful, but unproven. Through patrols, dangerous assignments, ordinary service, and their growing relationships in Hearthglen, they become familiar faces within the garrison and settlement.

The Crusade they encounter here should be disciplined, severe, and deeply shaped by war, but also understandable. Its soldiers fight real monsters. Its suspicion has saved lives. Its quartermasters feed isolated outposts, its healers tend the wounded, and its patrols disappear into country where the dead still hunt the living.

By the end of the act, the characters should be able to look at Hearthglen and think: **We belong here.**

## Running The Act

The four adventures in this act are **highlights from the player characters' service**, not their complete service record.

The characters have already completed their basic training when the campaign begins. They know Hearthglen, their immediate superiors, and the routines of garrison life. What is new is active field service.

Act I is intended to unfold over several weeks. The intervals below provide a suggested pace:

The timeline above provides a suggested pace for the act. Adjust it as needed, but keep the timing of major assignments uncertain from the players' perspective. New orders should arrive when they make sense in the fiction, not according to a schedule the players can predict.

Between major assignments, the characters continue serving in Hearthglen. They stand watch, drill, escort supplies, maintain equipment, help workers, recover from wounds, attend funerals, spend time with comrades, train, study magic, and pursue personal projects.

Most of this ordinary service should be handled narratively. The campaign tracks time in weeks so that training, equipment orders, crafting, recovery, and other long-term activities have a meaningful place in play without requiring the Gamemaster to account for every day.

### Weekly Routine

During weeks of routine service, the characters continue living and working in Hearthglen. These quieter periods give the campaign room to breathe between major assignments and help establish the sense that the characters are part of a functioning garrison and community.

Use these weeks for ordinary duties, training, patrols, recovery, equipment orders, personal projects, and social scenes. Characters may seek out friends, superiors, teachers, craftsmen, priests, or other people in Hearthglen, spend time with their comrades, or pursue goals of their own.

Some weeks may pass quickly in a few sentences. Others may fill much of a session if the characters have people they want to speak with, plans they want to pursue, or relationships they want to develop.

The weekly structure provides a steady rhythm for the act. Major adventures remain exceptional events, while life in Hearthglen continues between them.

### Training

During each full week of *routine service and downtime*, a player character may normally undertake **one period of focused training**.

\page

This can be used either for **skill training** or for **magical studies**, following the normal Dragonbane and Bane of Azeroth rules for the chosen activity. A character cannot normally do both during the same week.

This limit does not change the normal rules for training. It represents the practical limits of military service, available instructors, suitable facilities, and the time required for serious study.

### Patrol Duty

During each week of routine service, the characters are assigned **one regular patrol**. Roll once on the Patrol Events table to see whether anything noteworthy occurs.

The Gamemaster may also offer the group **one additional voluntary patrol during the same week**. If the characters accept, they patrol together and the Gamemaster makes another roll on the Patrol Events table. After completing the voluntary patrol, each player character may mark **one skill of their choice for advancement**. This advancement mark represents the additional practical experience gained through field duty and is separate from any training undertaken during the same week.

Extra patrols should arise naturally in the fiction as voluntary assignments offered to the group: an additional road patrol, a check on an outlying watch post, an escort that needs extra protection, or a sweep requested by the garrison. The players should not be told that such an opportunity automatically exists every week.

Regular patrols, voluntary patrols, and major adventures should enter play in much the same way: through orders, requests, duty rosters, or an officer seeking out the characters.

This helps ordinary service and important assignments feel like part of the same military routine. From the players' perspective, there should be no obvious signal that one set of orders is merely a patrol while another begins one of the act's major adventures.

A routine patrol may turn out to be uneventful, while an apparently ordinary assignment may become *The Missing Patrol* or *Grain for the Living*. Presenting them through the same channels helps preserve that uncertainty and keeps the characters focused on what their orders mean in the fiction rather than on whether they have just been handed a "main quest."

If desired, the Gamemaster can even make a patrol roll behind the screen when introducing a major assignment, simply to preserve the same table rhythm and avoid signaling that anything unusual is about to happen.

### Other Duties

Other duties do not normally require rolls. Guard shifts, drills, routine escorts, maintenance work, and uneventful assignments can usually be summarized in a sentence or two.

\column

A quiet week might be described as:

> The week passes without incident. You patrol the northern road, spend two miserable nights at an outlying watch post, and help escort a timber wagon back from the mills. By the end of the week, the rain has turned the training yard to mud.

Quiet service is important. If every patrol reveals a hidden horror and every wagon is attacked, danger quickly becomes routine. Hearthglen should have time to become familiar, useful, and worth protecting before later acts begin to change it.

## Shape of the Act

The four adventures gradually expand the characters' place in Hearthglen and their understanding of the war around them.


### Trouble at the Mill

The characters help ordinary people and deal with a genuine local danger.

The Crusade responds correctly. The source of contamination is isolated and destroyed, the mill can reopen, and people are safer because the characters acted. There is no conspiracy behind the danger. The Third War has simply left wounds that have not healed.

### The Missing Patrol

The characters encounter the ordinary cost of the war against the Scourge.

The missing soldiers were not fools, and Sergeant Beatrix did not recklessly throw their lives away. They encountered a threat, made a reasonable decision, and paid for it. The characters' task is not to uncover a secret. It is to find their comrades and bring back as many as they can.

### Grain for the Living

The characters see how much of the war is sustained by ordinary work.

Food, medicine, lamp oil, tools, horses, wagons, and people willing to move them through dangerous country keep isolated positions alive. Nothing about the shipment is sinister. It matters because people need it.

### Living Among the Dead

The characters are sent to rescue civilians stranded close to Scourge-held territory. Most are exactly what they appear to be: frightened people trying to survive. One of them is not.

The characters must decide how closely to question the survivors, which inconsistencies matter, and how much trust to place in people they have only just met. If Merek Calder escapes suspicion, the characters may unknowingly escort a Cult of the Damned agent directly into Hearthglen.

### The End of the Act

*Living Among the Dead* is the final major adventure of New Blood, but the act does not need to end the moment the characters return through Hearthglen's gates.

Allow the consequences to settle. A few days or weeks may pass. The rescued survivors find somewhere to sleep and begin deciding what remains of their lives. Reports are written. Equipment is cleaned. Patrols leave and return. Wagons arrive late. Someone complains about the weather. Hearthglen continues.

Then show the characters what has changed. Soldiers who once barely noticed them now greet them by name. Workers at the mills remember who made their road safe. Someone in the Common Hall saves them seats. Brenna complains to them as though they have always been part of her logistical problems. Durgen speaks to them as soldiers whose judgment he knows rather than recruits who must still prove themselves.

The player characters are no longer new blood. They have protected the people of Hearthglen, searched for missing comrades, kept an isolated position supplied, and faced an enemy who hid among innocent people. They have earned their place.

At the end of the act, **the player characters earn another heroic ability each**.

New duties will come soon enough, but for now, Hearthglen is home.

# Hearthglen
{title="Act 1"}

{{dropcap O}}nce a prosperous town of Lordaeron, Hearthglen now stands as one of the Scarlet Crusade’s strongest footholds in the Western Plaguelands. Its walls shelter soldiers, craftsmen, laborers, priests, and families who refuse to surrender the land entirely to the dead.

For the player characters, Hearthglen is more than a military post. It is home.

They receive orders here, recover between expeditions, repair equipment, trade news, make friends, and watch patrols leave through the gates. The town should feel disciplined and heavily defended, but also lived in. Hammers ring from the workshops, carts rattle through muddy streets, laundry hangs between old stone walls, soldiers drill in the yards, and smoke rises from kitchens and smithies.

The Scarlet Crusade is not merely occupying Hearthglen. It is keeping it alive.

## The Situation

Hearthglen lies north of Andorhal, surrounded by lands scarred by the plague of undeath. The Scourge remains a constant threat, and travel beyond the town's patrol routes is dangerous.

The settlement is centered on **Mardenholde Keep**, once the seat of Hearthglen's rulers and now the local center of Scarlet authority. **Taelan Fordring** holds Mardenholde and serves as Hearthglen's most prominent leader, but much of the settlement's daily military life is overseen by **High Protector Lorik** and the officers serving beneath him.

The town's population is a mixture of Scarlet soldiers and civilians. Some inhabitants were born in Lordaeron before its fall. Others have come more recently: survivors, refugees, craftsmen, laborers, and families attached to the Crusade.

Life here is hard, but not desperate. Fields and gardens survive where the soil allows it. Timber comes from nearby logging camps. Smiths repair weapons and tools. Storehouses hold food, arrows, lamp oil, medicine, and supplies bound for distant Scarlet positions. Priests care for the wounded and conduct services for the living and the dead.

The Crusade's discipline is visible everywhere. Gates are guarded, weapons are kept close, patrols move according to schedule, and strangers attract attention. The Crusade is vigilant, but not irrational. Hearthglen exists in hostile territory. People have survived because they are careful.

### Hearthglen in the Campaign

Hearthglen is the player characters' primary home base during the early campaign.

They can normally come here to:

- rest and recover,
- receive new orders,
- seek mundane healing and religious aid,
- repair or replace common equipment,
- speak with officers and specialists,
- gather local information,
- socialize with soldiers and civilians,
- arrange transport or supplies for missions,
- and reconnect with recurring NPCs.

The settlement should remain recognizable even as the campaign changes around it. Later adventures may alter attitudes, personnel, security measures, or the mood of particular locations, but this chapter describes Hearthglen in its normal state near the beginning of the campaign.

### Life In Hearthglen

Hearthglen is neither a peaceful town nor a fortress populated only by soldiers. Most inhabitants live according to routines shaped by war. Workers leave for the lumber camps under guard. Patrols depart at dawn and return after dark. Smiths repair armor damaged by undead claws. Priests bless the dead before burial or burning. Children know the sound of the alarm horns. Farmers and laborers can identify common Scourge creatures from a distance.

None of this prevents ordinary life. People argue over food, gamble after duty, complain about officers, fall in love, mend roofs, celebrate returning patrols, and complain about the weather.

For many, the Crusade is not an abstract ideology. It is the force that keeps the gates guarded and the dead outside.

### The Old Town

Signs of the old Hearthglen remain throughout the settlement. Some houses have been repaired and returned to use. Others stand roofless behind newer Scarlet buildings. Old Lordaeron stonework appears beneath fresh timber reinforcements. Faded shop signs remain above storerooms. A ruined family home may now serve as a guard post. These remnants should be present without turning every street into a memorial.

\column

### Discipline

Weapons are common, but violence within the settlement is not tolerated. Scarlet authority is clear and visible. Soldiers are expected to obey orders, remain properly equipped, attend assigned duties, and report signs of plague or undead activity immediately. Civilians are not formally part of the military hierarchy, but the distinction can become blurred in an isolated settlement where almost every able-bodied adult may be expected to help during an attack.

## Locations

### 1. Mardenholde Keep

> Mardenholde Keep rises above the surrounding town in weathered gray stone, older than most of the defenses built around it. Scarlet banners hang from repaired battlements, and armored guards watch the approach from above.
>
> Beneath the signs of war, the keep still carries traces of what it once was: the seat of a prosperous Lordaeron household rather than a fortress at the edge of a dead kingdom.

Mardenholde Keep is the political and military heart of Hearthglen.

It contains audience rooms, offices, meeting chambers, quarters for senior officers, records, and protected stores. Some portions are old Lordaeron construction, while others have been rebuilt or adapted by the Scarlet Crusade.

The player characters are unlikely to wander freely through the entire keep, but they may be summoned here for important orders, formal reports, ceremonies, or meetings with senior figures.

#### Highlord Taelan Fordring

is lord of Mardenholde and one of the most visible symbols of Scarlet authority in Hearthglen.

He should initially make a strong and largely positive impression.

Taelan is earnest, courageous, deeply committed to the defense of the living, and accustomed to responsibility. He does not need to be flawless, but he should feel like someone whom reasonable people might willingly follow.

He does not normally concern himself with routine patrol assignments or minor logistical matters. When Taelan speaks directly to newly recruited characters, it should feel significant.

#### High Protector Lorik

is responsible for much of Hearthglen's practical military security.

Where Taelan represents authority, Lorik exercises it.

He oversees patrol readiness, guards, local responses to threats, and the officers responsible for everyday operations. He is experienced, observant, and not easily impressed by bravado.

Lorik expects discipline but values competence. A soldier who notices something important, adapts intelligently, or admits uncertainty is more useful to him than one who blindly pretends everything is under control.

He can become one of the player characters' most important recurring superiors.

#### The Command Chamber

is where Hearthglen's senior officers gather to discuss matters that require more than routine attention. Reports are usually handled by junior officers elsewhere, but significant developments eventually reach Lorik or the officers assembled here.

Maps of the surrounding countryside cover one wall. Wooden markers indicate patrols, known undead concentrations, supply routes, and positions that have recently gone silent.

### 2. Barracks and Training Yard

> The training yard never seems entirely quiet. Wooden practice weapons crack against shields, instructors bark corrections, and armored soldiers circle one another in the churned earth.
>
> Along one side of the yard stand long timber barracks, their doors open to the smell of leather, lamp oil, damp wool, and too many people sleeping in too little space.

Most Scarlet soldiers stationed in Hearthglen live or spend part of their day here.

The barracks are practical rather than comfortable. Equipment racks line the walls, personal possessions are kept in small chests, and duty rosters are posted where everyone can see them.

#### Cavalier Durgen

frequently operates between Hearthglen and the surrounding roads, towers, and patrol routes.

Durgen is closer to the daily life of rank-and-file soldiers than Lorik. He may lead field patrols, escort missions, scouting sweeps, or rapid responses to threats beyond the walls.

He is a useful NPC when the player characters need an officer who may actually accompany them into danger.

Durgen should be competent enough that his presence is reassuring without turning him into the solution to the player characters' problems.

#### Erica Vane

is a fellow recruit and one of the recurring faces in the barracks. She is a recent recruit of roughly the same standing as the player characters.

Erica is capable but inexperienced. She is eager to prove that she belongs and initially believes strongly in the Crusade's mission without being especially ideological about it. She can train beside the player characters, share meals with them, complain about assignments, celebrate victories, and occasionally be sent on different missions.

Erica's importance is not what she knows. It is that she begins the campaign standing approximately where

### 3. The Chapel

> Warm candlelight spills from narrow windows set into an old stone building. The Scarlet flame hangs above the entrance, but the chapel beneath it is older than the Crusade.
>
> Inside, rows of simple benches face a modest altar. Names have been carved into wooden plaques along the walls. There are too many plaques.

The chapel serves soldiers and civilians alike.

Services are held regularly, but people also come here privately to pray, mourn, seek counsel, or sit somewhere quiet.

The faith practiced here should initially feel sincere rather than theatrical. The people of Hearthglen have buried friends and family for years. Religion offers discipline and certainty, but also comfort.

#### High Clerist Mara Voss

oversees much of Hearthglen's spiritual care. Mara is stern in public and patient in private. She has spent years treating soldiers who returned from battles they should not have survived and consoling families whose relatives did not return at all. She believes with absolution that undeath is an abomination. That conviction does not make her cruel.

At the beginning of the campaign she distinguishes clearly between protecting the living and punishing them.

### 4. The Infirmary

> The infirmary smells of boiled cloth, herbs, blood, and strong spirits. Beds stand in close rows beneath high windows, with curtains hung between the worst cases.
>
> A pair of orderlies move quietly from patient to patient while a Scarlet priest washes dried blood from her hands.

The infirmary occupies a building close to the chapel. It treats combat wounds, accidents, disease, exposure, and injuries suffered by civilians working outside the walls. Wounded patrols returning to Hearthglen are usually brought here first.

High Clerist Mara Voss divides her time between the chapel and infirmary, assisted by Scarlet priests, orderlies, and anyone with useful medical skills.

#### Plague Precautions

are a routine part of life in Hearthglen. Anyone suspected of exposure to unusual disease or Scourge corruption is isolated until examined.

At the beginning of the campaign, these measures should feel careful and practical rather than fanatical. The distinction may become important later.

### 5. Smithy and Workshops

> Hammer blows ring from a cluster of workshops built against an old stone wall. Sparks leap from an open forge while workers move between racks of spearheads, horseshoes, nails, hinges, axe blades, and pieces of damaged armor.
>
> Not everything made here is a weapon. In Hearthglen, a broken wagon wheel can be as dangerous as a broken sword.

The workshops support both the garrison and the civilian population. The local smiths and other craftsmen repair weapons, armor, tools, carts, doors, and fittings needed throughout the settlement.

Common equipment can normally be repaired here, subject to available materials. More unusual items may require supplies from elsewhere.

#### Work Before Glory

is visible throughout Hearthglen's workshops. They are a good place to remind the players that the Crusade is an army sustained by ordinary labor. A blacksmith may spend the morning repairing a paladin's breastplate and the afternoon replacing iron bands on a grain cart.

### 6. Storehouses

> Carts stand wheel to wheel inside a broad fenced yard. Soldiers and laborers carry sacks between long storehouses while clerks check crates against wax tablets and ink-stained ledgers.
>
> Someone is always shouting about missing rope.

Food, weapons, ammunition, medical supplies, lamp oil, spare tools, clothing, timber fittings, and countless other necessities pass through the quartermaster's yard.

This is one of the least glamorous and most important places in Hearthglen.

#### Scarlet Quartermaster Brenna Holt

is responsible for keeping track of what Hearthglen has, what it needs, and where it is going. Brenna has little patience for soldiers who think supplies simply appear because an officer requested them. She is one of the best sources of practical information in Hearthglen and is also the natural recurring contact for supply and convoy missions.

#### Supplies

for official assignments can normally be obtained from the quartermaster, provided the request is reasonable and appropriate to the task. Personal purchases and unusual equipment are another matter. Brenna records everything.

### 7. The Lumber Mills

> The smell of fresh-cut pine carries well before the mills come into view. Saws rasp through timber, axes strike in steady rhythm, and teams of workers drag stripped trunks toward stacks waiting for transport.
>
> Scarlet guards are present, but most of the people here carry tools rather than weapons.

Several logging sites and mills outside the main defenses provide Hearthglen with timber. The work is dangerous even without the Scourge. Falling trees, heavy machinery, wild animals, and travel beyond the safest patrol routes all cause injuries.

The mills produce material direly needed for palisades, roofs, repairs, carts, barricades, fuel, watch platforms, and military construction. The workers are therefore an essential part of Hearthglen's survival.

#### Huntsman Radley

spends much of her time beyond Hearthglen's walls. She tracks dangerous animals, scouts routes, investigates unusual signs in the wilderness, and helps patrols distinguish ordinary dangers from Scourge activity.

#### Bloodshot
is rarely far from Radley. He is well trained, alert, and deeply accustomed to working at her side. Radley trusts his reactions, especially when scent or instinct reveals something others have missed.

### 8. Gates and Watch Posts

> The South Gate stands between reinforced towers built from old stone and newer timber. Guards watch the road from above while travelers wait below to be questioned before entering.
>
> Beyond the walls, the road disappears into country where smoke on the horizon is never assumed to be harmless.

Hearthglen has two gates, each serving a different part of the settlement's daily traffic.

#### The South Gate

is Hearthglen's main entrance and faces the road toward Andorhal and the rest of the Western Plaguelands. Most travelers, military traffic, and supplies arriving from the south pass through here. Guards watch the road from reinforced towers and question strangers before allowing them inside.

#### The North Gate

is sometimes called the Mill Gate. It is smaller than the South Gate but still wide enough for wagons and serves the lumber mills, nearby forests, and northern patrol routes. Timber carts, work crews, hunters, and patrols pass through regularly, usually under guard.

Both gates are controlled at all times. Known residents and Scarlet patrols pass with little difficulty. Strangers are questioned, watched, and sometimes searched.

This vigilance reflects genuine danger. Scourge creatures, Cult of the Damned agents, bandits, infected travelers, and desperate refugees may all approach the settlement.

#### Patrols

operate along the roads, lumber routes, nearby watch posts, and the safer approaches to Hearthglen.

Patrols that fail to report on time are taken seriously. Anyone entering or leaving Hearthglen through the gates is recorded by the guards on duty. The gates are natural locations for departures, returning expeditions, wounded patrols, visitors, rumors from outside, and sudden alarms.

#### Alarm Procedures

are practiced regularly. When a recognized alarm signal is sounded, the gates are closed and off-duty soldiers report to their assigned positions. Civilians know where they are expected to go. The routine is practiced often enough that panic is rare.

### 9. Hearthglen Proper

> Hearthglen's streets wind between repaired stone houses, newer timber buildings, workshops, small yards, and occasional blackened ruins that no one has bothered to clear.
>
> A woman shakes dust from a blanket above the street. Two children chase one another around a water barrel until a passing soldier tells them to move. Somewhere nearby, someone is arguing loudly about onions.

Most of Hearthglen's civilian life takes place here. There is no clean division between a military district and a civilian one. Soldiers rent rooms from families. Craftsmen work for the Crusade. Widows prepare food for patrols. Children carry messages for people who give them sweets. Small gardens occupy whatever usable soil can be found. Old cellars are used for storage. Abandoned buildings have been stripped for stone and timber. Some ruins are left untouched because no one has the time or desire to rebuild them.

#### Civilians

help make Hearthglen feel like a place people actually live in rather than a collection of military locations.

They complain about food prices, bad weather, delayed repairs, officers they dislike, patrols that block the road, and whether the latest roof work will survive the winter. They gossip about distant battles, argue about refugees, speculate about new recruits, and notice which soldiers return from patrol and which do not.

Most support the Scarlet Crusade because they see its soldiers protecting Hearthglen every day. Others are less interested in the Crusade as an institution and are simply trying to keep their families fed, their homes standing, and some part of ordinary life intact.

### 10. THE COMMON HALL

> The common hall is loud long before the door opens. Inside, long tables are crowded with soldiers, workers, teamsters, craftsmen, and anyone else who has earned enough coin or favor for a hot meal and a drink.
>
> Wet cloaks steam near the hearth. Someone has started singing badly. Someone else is losing money at dice.

The common hall is Hearthglen's main informal gathering place. It may once have been an inn, guildhall, or large merchant house before the fall. Today its original purpose matters less than the fact that it has space, a kitchen, a hearth, and enough tables for a crowd.

Scarlet officers do not officially conduct business here, but in practice a great deal of useful information passes through the room.

#### Food and Drink

are simple and filling rather than memorable. The common hall usually serves stew, bread, preserved meat, root vegetables, weak ale, and whatever else Hearthglen can currently spare.

The quality varies with supply. A particularly good meal is noticed, while shortages become a topic of conversation almost immediately.

#### Rumors

spread easily through the common hall, making it one of the best places to introduce ordinary news without turning every conversation into a formal briefing.

Soldiers and civilians talk about patrols returning late, dangerous stretches of a road, strange lights near Andorhal, upcoming convoys, transfers to the Scarlet Monastery, missing relatives, and whatever mundane gossip is currently circulating through Hearthglen.

Most of these stories should remain exactly that: rumors, complaints, misunderstandings, or fragments of ordinary life. Not every conversation needs to lead to an adventure.

#### Familiar Faces

make the common hall useful as a recurring social location.

Erica Vane often spends time here when off duty, while Durgen may sit down with soldiers after returning from patrol. Radley appears less predictably, sometimes with Bloodshot asleep beneath the table.

Lorik rarely stays long, and Taelan almost never visits casually. Their presence should therefore feel more significant when it does occur.

## Goods and Services

Hearthglen is not a major market town, but it is a functioning military settlement supplied well enough to support soldiers, craftsmen, workers, and their families.

Most **Common** equipment from the Dragonbane and Bane of Azeroth equipment lists can normally be obtained here when it makes sense for a working settlement or active garrison to have it. Everyday provisions, ordinary weapons and armor, ammunition, tools, clothing, medical supplies, and similar necessities are therefore rarely difficult to find.

Availability does not necessarily mean that an item is sitting on a shop shelf. Equipment may come from the quartermaster's stores, a local craftsman, a trader, or a commission that takes time to complete.

For **Uncommon**, **Rare**, or otherwise specialized equipment, the Gamemaster should consider whether Hearthglen has a reason to produce, stock, or import it. Unusual designs, foreign equipment, rare materials, luxury goods, and specialist tools may require time, a particular contact, or supplies brought in from elsewhere.

If Hearthglen cannot provide something, the characters can usually learn where it might be found or ordered.

### Service Issue

Characters are not normally expected to pay for ordinary supplies required to carry out official duties.

Food for the road, ammunition, torches, lamp oil, rope, basic medical supplies, camping equipment, and similar expendable necessities can usually be issued when appropriate to an assignment.

More valuable resources are treated differently. Weapons, armor, mounts, expensive equipment, and unusual items remain Scarlet Crusade property and are issued only when there is a reason for the characters to need them.

Military stores exist to support the characters' duties, not to provide free personal upgrades.

### Repairs and Orders

Hearthglen's craftsmen can repair most ordinary equipment and produce many Common items to order. Equipment that cannot be made locally may instead be brought in with a suitable supply shipment from another Scarlet settlement.

How long this takes depends on the item, the work required, current demand on Hearthglen's workshops, and whether the necessary materials are already available.

## Magical Study

Hearthglen is a military settlement rather than a great center of learning, but the garrison contains enough experienced soldiers, priests, mages, craftsmen, and specialists for the characters to continue developing their skills between assignments.

Skill training follows the normal Dragonbane rules. Suitable instructors can usually be found for skills that have an obvious place in the life of the settlement, particularly combat, riding, healing, wilderness skills, crafting, and other practical disciplines.

More unusual fields of study may require the right individual, suitable material, or instruction elsewhere.

### Learning Magic

Characters learn new magic using the normal Dragonbane rules. During a full week of routine service and downtime, a character may normally devote their focused training period to magical studies. As described in *Running the Act*, this is normally limited to one period of focused training per week, whether it is used for skill training or magical study.

Hearthglen can normally provide instruction in **level 1 and level 2 spells** from the schools represented in the settlement. This is a limit on the instruction routinely available here, not on the abilities of individual NPCs.

A teacher does not automatically know every spell belonging to a school they practice. The Gamemaster should consider whether a suitable teacher or grimoire is available, whether the spell fits that teacher's knowledge, and whether any prerequisites for learning it are fulfilled.

If a particular spell cannot be taught in Hearthglen, an instructor may still be able to point the character toward someone or somewhere that can provide further study.

**Level 3 spells** are beyond Hearthglen's normal teaching resources. Characters seeking that level of instruction will generally need to continue their studies at the Scarlet Monastery or through another suitably accomplished teacher.

**Level 4 and level 5 spells** are not routinely available at the campaign's major settlements. Such knowledge is normally found through exceptional teachers, rare grimoires, forgotten archives, or other discoveries made during play.

### Magic in Hearthglen

The following schools are represented among Hearthglen's priests, mages, and other specialists:

**General Magic** can be studied through any suitable spellcaster with the necessary knowledge.

**Animism** is practiced through the traditions of the Holy Light. Instruction is readily associated with healing, protection, purification, and magic used against the undead.

**Elementalism** is practiced by battle magi serving with the garrison. Their teaching emphasizes practical battlefield magic, including fire, frost, wind, and similar effects.

**Mentalism** is part of Hearthglen's arcane tradition and can be studied through its trained mages.

## Bringing It to Life

Hearthglen works best when it feels like a place the characters live in rather than somewhere they visit between adventures. The following advice can help establish that sense of familiarity and give the people, routines, and relationships of the settlement room to develop during the act.

### Let Hearthglen Become Home

The recurring people of Hearthglen should appear outside the adventures in which they are immediately useful.

* Cavalier Durgen can be seen overseeing drills, eating with soldiers, arguing over patrol assignments, or returning exhausted from a mission of his own.

* Quartermaster Brenna Holt has wagons to account for whether the player characters are involved or not.

* Huntsman Radley and Bloodshot come and go from the surrounding countryside.

* High Clerist Mara Voss tends more wounded people than just the characters.

* Initiate Erica Vane trains, works, complains, celebrates, and fails alongside her peers.

Let these people occasionally exist without asking anything of the player characters. Someone may simply share a table with them. Someone may wave from across the yard. Someone they helped weeks ago may remember their name. These small moments are what turn a collection of NPCs into a community.

### Recurring Faces

Do not seed every conversation with clues, warnings, or future plot. For now, many of Hearthglen's people are simply comrades. Give important recurring NPCs enough ordinary table time that the players form their own opinions of them. Those relationships can become valuable later, but they should feel real before they become useful to the story.

### Let the Characters Have a Routine

As Hearthglen becomes familiar, let the players decide how their characters spend some of their quieter time.

They may develop favorite places, seek out particular people, establish habits, or return to conversations without waiting for the Gamemaster to prompt them. A place begins to feel like home when the characters choose to spend time there even when no adventure requires it.

### Trust, Not Reputation

There is no single numerical measure of the characters' standing within the Scarlet Crusade. People remember what the characters have done.

Quartermaster Brenna Holt may trust someone who saved a wagon others would have abandoned. High Clerist Mara Voss may remember who remained with a dying soldier. Cavalier Durgen may respect a character's judgment in the field even when another officer considers the same character undisciplined.

Access to instruction, equipment, records, or authority should therefore depend on circumstances and relationships rather than a universal reputation score.

Trust is personal. So is distrust.

### Establish the Baseline

Act I should not make the Scarlet Crusade look secretly monstrous. Let its precautions work. Let competent officers make sensible decisions. Let soldiers risk themselves for civilians and comrades. When suspicion is justified, allow it to be justified. Later developments carry more weight if the player characters first understand why people in Hearthglen came to believe that vigilance, discipline, and harsh measures were necessary.

### Give Normality Room to Breathe

The adventures in this act represent the moments the characters will remember. Between them lie days and weeks of ordinary military life.

Do not rush these quieter periods simply because nothing dramatic is happening. The stronger Hearthglen feels as a functioning home and community now, the more meaningful later changes to the Crusade and its people will become.

# Trouble at the Mill
{title="Act 1"}

{{dropcap O}}he lumber mills outside Hearthglen supply the Scarlet Crusade with timber for palisades, repairs, carts, fuel, and new construction. When workers at one of the mills are attacked by unusually aggressive wildlife, work comes to a halt. The player characters, newly recruited

\column

into the Crusade, are ordered to make the area safe.

What first appears to be a simple matter of driving off dangerous animals leads to a forgotten storehouse and a grim remnant of Lordaeron's fall.

## The Situation

Several days ago, a group of lumber workers noticed a large bear prowling unusually close to the mill. At first they ignored it. Wild animals are common in the forests around Hearthglen, and hungry predators occasionally venture near settled areas.

The following day the bear attacked one of the workers without warning. The man survived, badly mauled, after his companions drove the animal away with axes and burning brands. Since then, more tracks have been found around the lumber yard and another worker claims to have seen a second animal moving through the trees. The mill foreman has halted work until the area is safe.

The bears are not undead and are not being controlled by the Scourge. They have been feeding from an old cache of provisions hidden in a forgotten storehouse in the forest. The storehouse dates from before the fall of Hearthglen. Among the abandoned supplies are several crates of grain and other foodstuffs shipped from **Andorhal** shortly before the plague of undeath swept through Lordaeron. The contents have long since rotted, but the corruption that once tainted them remains. The bears broke into the storehouse while scavenging for food. What they consumed has left them diseased, maddened, and unusually aggressive.

If the player characters simply kill the animals and return to Hearthglen, **Huntsman Radley** recognizes from their report that the behavior is abnormal. Killing the bears may remove the immediate danger, but it does not explain why healthy animals suddenly began approaching and attacking the mill. If necessary, Radley urges them to return and find the source.

{{pageNumber,auto Act 1 --- Trouble at the Mill}}
\page

There is no conspiracy behind the incident. No Scarlet officer is behaving suspiciously, no Cult of the Damned agent is involved, and the discovery has no connection to Grand Crusader Dathrohan. This is simply one of the many old wounds that Lordaeron has not yet finished bleeding from.

## The Assignment

The player characters receive their orders from a duty officer in Hearthglen, acting under the authority of the local command.

The matter is presented plainly. Work at one of the lumber mills has stopped after an animal attack. Timber is too important to Hearthglen's defenses for the mill to remain idle. The player characters are ordered to investigate the area, deal with any dangerous animals, and ensure that the workers can return safely.

The officer knows little more than this:

- One worker was badly injured by a bear.
- The attack happened close to the mill.
- Several workers claim that more animals have been seen nearby.
- Nobody has been killed.
- The foreman refuses to resume work until the area has been cleared.

# The Missing Patrol
{title="Act 1"}

{{dropcap S}}outh of Hearthglen, the old roads of Lordaeron pass abandoned farms, ruined cottages, and fields that have not seen a proper harvest since the plague. Scarlet patrols still guard these routes, watching for Scourge movement and keeping the dead away from the roads used by workers, couriers, and supply wagons. When one such patrol fails to return, the player characters are sent to find them.

What begins as a search for overdue comrades leads to an abandoned farmstead, a gathering of ghouls, and a reminder that even experienced soldiers can get overwhelmed.

## The Situation

A five-person Scarlet patrol led by **Sergeant Alexandria Beatrix** left Hearthglen the previous morning to inspect a small watch post and the road beyond it. The patrol was expected back before nightfall, but by dawn, there has still been no sign of them.

The patrol reached the watch post without incident. From there, they spotted fresh ghoul tracks leading east toward an abandoned farmstead. The ghouls appeared to be dragging corpses and carrion away from the road. Beatrix decided to investigate.

The patrol caught the ghouls at the farmstead and attacked. They killed several, but more emerged from the surrounding fields and ruined buildings, forcing the patrol apart. Beatrix was badly wounded in the fighting, her face brutally maimed, but managed to reach the old farmhouse with two of her soldiers. They barricaded themselves inside and have been holding there ever since.

The remaining two soldiers were ordered to retreat and report back to Hearthglen. The ghouls pursued them. One soldier was killed before they reached the road, while the other, **Tomas Rell**, was badly wounded but managed to escape. He eventually collapsed in a ditch beside the road, where he has remained hidden.

The ghouls are using the abandoned farmstead as a feeding and gathering place, dragging bodies there from the surrounding countryside. If left alone, their range will eventually spread toward the roads used by workers and supply wagons.

# Grain for the Living
{title="Act 1"}

{{dropcap R}}oads keep Hearthglen alive as surely as walls and swords do. Food, medicine, lamp oil, tools, ammunition, and replacement equipment must constantly move between the Crusade's settlements, patrol posts, and forward positions across the Western Plaguelands. Most of these journeys are uneventful, but that does not make them unimportant.

In this adventure, the player characters are assigned to escort a small supply convoy from Hearthglen to a fortified waystation along the road toward Andorhal. The mission is straightforward: keep the wagons moving and deliver the supplies to the soldiers who need them.

## The Situation

Quartermaster **Brenna Holt** is preparing two supply wagons for a Scarlet waystation south of Hearthglen.

The waystation supports patrols operating along the road toward Andorhal and provides shelter for couriers, wounded soldiers, and civilians travelling under Scarlet protection. Its stores are running low after a previous delivery was delayed and another convoy was diverted elsewhere.

The wagons carry ordinary but necessary supplies: grain and other preserved food, lamp oil, bandages and simple medicines, arrows and crossbow bolts, spare tools, replacement clothing and blankets, and a small amount of routine military equipment.

The road is normally considered manageable, but recent rain has damaged several sections. One of the convoy's regular guards is also recovering from an injury, leaving the escort short-handed.

The greatest danger is not a planned attack. It is the accumulated risk of moving heavy wagons through damaged country where help may be hours away.

During the journey, the player characters may have to deal with poor road conditions, damaged equipment, delays, signs of nearby Scourge activity, and the possibility of undead being drawn toward the convoy.

The mission succeeds if enough of the supplies reach the waystation for it to continue operating.

The player characters are not expected to preserve every crate at the cost of lives.

# Living Among the Dead
{title="Act 1"}

{{dropcap T}}he closer one travels toward Andorhal, the fewer signs of ordinary life remain. Abandoned farms line the old roads, fields have gone untended for years, and Scourge patrols move through places that once connected one village to the next.

When a Scarlet reconnaissance patrol spots smoke rising from an isolated farmstead, they discover something increasingly rare in the Plaguelands: living civilians.

One soldier is sent back to Hearthglen on horseback with the report. The player characters are assigned to return with him, find the survivors, and bring them to safety if they can.

However, not everyone waiting at the farmstead is what they claim to be.

## The Situation

A Scarlet reconnaissance patrol has been observing Scourge movement along the western approaches to Andorhal. Their orders are to remain concealed, record enemy activity, and avoid engagements that might reveal their position.

During the patrol, they spotted smoke rising from an abandoned cluster of farm buildings some distance from the road. Later, they saw several living people moving between the buildings.

The patrol could not abandon its assignment to investigate. Instead, its commander detached one mounted soldier to carry the report back to Hearthglen. The rest remain in the field.

The survivors number eight people. Most are ordinary refugees who have been hiding in the ruins for several weeks after their previous shelter was overrun. They have little food left and know that remaining this close to Andorhal is becoming increasingly dangerous.

One of them, **Merek Calder**, is secretly a member of the Cult of the Damned.

Merek joined the group shortly before they reached the farmstead, claiming to be a lone traveler fleeing the Scourge. He quickly made himself useful. He knows which roads to avoid, where undead patrols are likely to pass, and where usable food can still be found.

The survivors believe this knowledge has helped keep them alive.

{{pageNumber,auto Act 1 --- Living Among the Dead}}
\page

In truth, Merek has been receiving information from another cult contact and has deliberately guided the group through areas where the Scourge are unlikely to interfere with them. Keeping the survivors alive serves his purpose: travelling among genuine refugees gives him a convincing identity and makes him far less suspicious than a lone stranger emerging from Scourge-held territory.

Merek hopes eventually to reach Scarlet-controlled territory with the group. If he can enter Hearthglen as one of several rescued civilians, he will be in a position to observe patrol routines, defenses, supply movements, and other information of value to the Cult while waiting for further instructions.

For that reason, Merek has no reason to resist rescue. He cooperates with the player characters, encourages the other survivors to do the same, and may appear to be one of the most helpful people in the group. If his cover holds, being escorted to Hearthglen is exactly what he wants.

The player characters may discover Merek through inconsistencies in his story, physical evidence, or signs that his knowledge of Scourge movements is too precise to be explained by ordinary experience.

The other seven survivors have committed no crime. Scarlet procedure is to check them for signs of plague or corruption, question them about where they have been, and escort them to Hearthglen if they can be safely moved. Anyone presenting a genuine threat is to be detained separately.

## The Assignment

The player characters receive the assignment from **High Protector Lorik** or an officer acting on his orders. The Scarlet soldier who carried the original report, **Corin Hale** is present and will accompany them back to the area.

The briefing is straightforward.

> *"A reconnaissance patrol found civilians west of Andorhal. They could not leave their position to investigate, so they sent word instead. Go with their rider, find these people, and bring them back if it is safe to do so. If the situation is worse than it appears, come back and tell us."*{color:var(--demongreen)}

Lorik can provide the following information:

- A group of civilians was seen sheltering in abandoned farm buildings.
- The patrol saw perhaps ten people moving around the farmstead.
- The reconnaissance patrol did not approach closely enough to speak with the group.
- The farmstead lies some distance from Andorhal itself, but Scourge patrols operate in the surrounding area.
- Corin Hale knows the route and can guide the player characters to the site.
- The player characters are not expected to engage a significant Scourge force. This is an extraction mission.
- Anyone showing signs of plague or Scourge corruption should be isolated and brought back for examination if possible.

Lorik gives no indication that he expects the survivors to be anything but living civilians in dangerous territory. The immediate concern is getting them out before the Scourge finds them.

- **What He Thinks of the Others:** Garrick is protective of Edda but rarely says so. He respects Alda's willingness to work, feels sorry for Hal, and tolerates Oren mostly because Edda does. He trusts Merek more readily than Edda does because Merek's advice has repeatedly kept people alive.
- **What He Hides:** Garrick took a small sack of grain from an abandoned cottage several days ago. He believes it may have belonged to other survivors and is ashamed that hunger made the decision easy.
- **Personality:** Patient, dependable, and slow to anger. Once angry, he becomes extremely direct and has little patience for argument.

### Hal Brenner

Hal Brenner is a young man who looks several years younger than he actually is. His slight build and boyish features make him easy to mistake for someone barely out of adolescence. He tries hard to make himself useful. He fetches water, repairs shutters, carries supplies, and regularly volunteers for work others are too tired to do.

He becomes visibly nervous around armed soldiers and tends to answer questions too quickly, often adding details nobody asked for.

- **What Hal Knows:** Hal knows that Edda has been rationing food more tightly than she admits and that Garrick has been helping her hide it from the others. He has seen Alda wake from nightmares after taking watch, knows that Brendan sometimes seeks him out when Elise is asleep, and has noticed Oren searching through abandoned buildings whenever the group stops. Hal was already travelling with the survivors when Merek joined them and remembers that Merek's clothes and supplies seemed unusually well kept for someone who claimed to have been alone for several days.
- **What He Thinks of the Others:** Hal admires Edda and is slightly intimidated by Garrick. He likes Brendan and feels protective of him. Alda makes him nervous because she asks direct questions. He does not trust Oren around unattended belongings. He is uncertain about Merek, but knows the group would likely have lost people without his guidance.
- **What He Hides:** Hal served in a local militia that collapsed during a Scourge attack. He fled when the line broke and has convinced himself that this makes him a deserter. He fears Scarlet soldiers will see it the same way.
- **Personality:** Helpful, anxious, conscientious, and eager to please. The harder someone questions him, the guiltier he tends to look.

### Merek Calder

Merek Calder presents himself as a capable traveller who survived alone before joining the group. He is calm, helpful, and rarely the first person to speak, but readily offers practical advice once others have begun discussing a problem.

He has deliberately made himself valuable. He scouts, finds food, identifies safer routes, helps with work around the farmstead, and treats the other survivors with enough genuine consideration that several of them have come to trust him.

- **What Merek Knows:** Merek has paid close attention to everyone in the group. He knows about Elise's cough, suspects Hal has some kind of military past, has seen Oren's collection of valuables, and knows that Garrick took the grain. He is aware that Alda distrusts the precision of his knowledge and that Brendan has seen him leave the farmstead alone.
- **What He Thinks of the Others:** Merek considers Edda the person most likely to notice if something does not add up and therefore treats her opinions with respect. He knows Garrick values results, Elise responds strongly to anyone helping Brendan, and Hal can easily be frightened into saying too much. He views Oren and Alda as the greatest risks to his cover.
- **What He Claims:** Merek says he survived by travelling carefully, observing Scourge patrols from a distance, and learning which roads and settlements were safest.
- **What He Hides:** Merek is an agent of the Cult of the Damned. A cult contact has been providing him with information about Scourge movements, allowing him to guide the survivors through areas where they are unlikely to be attacked.
- **What He Wants:** Merek wants to reach Hearthglen with the survivors. Entering Scarlet-held territory as one of several rescued civilians gives him a credible identity and an opportunity to gather information about patrols, defenses, and supply movements.
- **If Suspected:** Merek does not panic simply because someone questions him. He knows that almost everyone in the group has something they would rather keep hidden and can truthfully point toward several suspicious details that have nothing to do with the Cult.
- **If Exposed:** If arrest becomes inevitable, Merek first tries to talk his way out of the situation. He resorts to flight or violence only when he believes his cover cannot be preserved.
- **Personality:** Patient, attentive, understated, and skilled at making himself useful without appearing eager for influence.

### Oren Pike

Oren is a thin older man who walks with a stick after an injury that predates the plague. Before Lordaeron fell, he travelled between villages repairing household tools, locks, and simple mechanisms.

He has an excellent memory and an unfortunate habit of correcting other people's details, even when the correction does not matter.

- **What Oren Knows:** Oren remembers where and roughly when each member joined the group. He knows that Hal has changed parts of his story more than once, has noticed Elise hiding her cough, and knows that Garrick did not find the grain where he claims. He has also caught Brendan near his belongings and suspects the boy has seen his hidden valuables. Some of Merek's descriptions of earlier journeys do not quite match Oren's memory of the roads and settlements involved.
- **What He Thinks of the Others:** Oren respects Edda but enjoys arguing with her. He thinks Garrick is honest except when ashamed, considers Alda too quick to act, and finds Hal suspicious largely because Hal is so obviously frightened of questioning. He regards Merek as useful but too polished for someone who claims to have survived alone for long.
- **What He Hides:** Oren carries rings, lockets, coins, and other small valuables taken from abandoned homes and from bodies encountered during the journey. He insists the owners were already dead and sees little sense in leaving useful things behind, but knows how it will look if the items are discovered.
- **Personality:** Pedantic, skeptical, irritable, and sharp-minded. He can be unexpectedly funny, usually when he is not trying to be.

## The Investigation

There is no single clue that proves Merek Calder is a member of the Cult of the Damned.

Most of the survivors have something they would rather not discuss, and several of them can appear suspicious under questioning. The player characters are therefore more likely to uncover the truth by comparing stories, noticing patterns, and deciding which inconsistencies actually matter.

Merek's greatest protection is that he has genuinely helped the group survive.

### Talking to the Survivors

The survivors do not need to be formally interrogated for useful information to emerge. Ordinary conversation about where they came from, when they joined the group, and how they survived can reveal contradictions, private grievances, and observations about one another.

The Gamemaster should use the information in each survivor's profile freely. A character may volunteer something, mention it accidentally, reveal it when pressed, or point toward another survivor when attention becomes uncomfortable.

Not every secret is relevant to Merek.

- **Edda** has been hiding how little food remains.
- **Garrick** took grain that may have belonged to other survivors.
- **Elise** is concealing a persistent cough.
- **Alda** abandoned wounded companions during an earlier Scourge attack.
- **Oren** has taken valuables from abandoned homes and bodies.
- **Hal** fears being treated as a deserter.
- **Brendan** has noticed things the adults do not realize he has seen.

These secrets may create suspicion, but none of them connects the survivor to the Cult of the Damned.

### Merek's Story

Merek claims that he survived alone before joining the group by travelling carefully, watching the roads, and learning where the undead tend to move.

Much of what he says is plausible.

The problem is not that any one part of his story is impossible, but that his knowledge is consistently more accurate than it should be.

Several details can raise questions:

- Merek was the last survivor to join the group, and nobody can confirm where he was before that.

{{pageNumber,auto Act 1 --- Living Among the Dead}}
\page

- Hal remembers that Merek appeared unusually well supplied for someone who claimed to have travelled alone for several days.
- Alda knows the local roads and has occasionally been surprised by how certain Merek is about recent Scourge movement.
- Elise and Brendan know that Merek sometimes leaves the farmstead alone.
- Oren can identify small inconsistencies in Merek's descriptions of places he claims to have travelled through.
- Merek has repeatedly warned the group away from danger before there were obvious signs that danger was present.

None of these observations proves anything by itself.

### Too Much Knowledge

Merek's knowledge of the Scourge is the strongest thread available to the player characters.

His explanations are reasonable: tracks, distant movement, scavenger behavior, smoke, silence, and experience can all reveal danger to someone who knows what to look for.

What is harder to explain is how often he has been right.

If the player characters compare Merek's knowledge with Alda's or question him closely about how he reached a particular conclusion, they may realize that he is sometimes describing certainty where an ordinary survivor should only have suspicion.

The road back may reinforce this. If Merek warns the group away from the northern route and they later discover that a Scourge force was waiting there, his advice is proven correct. That makes him more useful, but it may also make him more suspicious.

### Following the Trail

Merek does not carry an obvious Cult symbol, written orders, or any other object that immediately exposes him.

If the player characters investigate his movements instead of his belongings, however, they may find evidence that he has been leaving the farmstead for reasons he has not fully explained.

Tracks, observations from Brendan or Elise, or careful questioning may establish that Merek has made several solitary trips away from the group.

Merek claims that he was scouting for food, water, and signs of undead movement.

That explanation is partly true.

The Gamemaster may allow further investigation outside the farmstead to reveal a place where Merek has met or exchanged information with his contact: disturbed ground, repeated footprints, an unusual marker, or signs that two people have met there recently.

Such evidence shows that Merek has concealed something, but does not necessarily reveal who he met or why.

### Pressing Merek

Merek does not panic simply because the player characters become suspicious.

He answers questions calmly, admits harmless truths where doing so makes him more believable, and points out that almost everyone in the group has something to hide.

If challenged about leaving the farmstead, he says that scouting alone is quieter and safer.

If challenged about his knowledge of Scourge movements, he credits caution, observation, and experience.

If challenged about inconsistencies in his past, he claims exhaustion, fear, or poor memory.

All of these explanations are plausible.

Merek becomes concerned only when the player characters begin combining several independent pieces of information or investigating his movements directly.

### When Merek Knows He Is in Danger

As long as suspicion remains vague, Merek stays with the group. Reaching Hearthglen remains his goal.

If the player characters openly accuse him without convincing evidence, he denies it and may receive support from survivors whose lives he has helped save.

If they uncover enough to make arrest likely, Merek begins looking for an opportunity to escape.

He prefers flight to violence. Violence draws attention, threatens his cover, and may turn the survivors against him.

If escape becomes impossible, he fights only as a last resort.

### Resolution

Merek does not need to be exposed for the adventure to reach a conclusion.

If the player characters accept the survivors' stories and escort all eight safely to Hearthglen, they have completed the assignment they were given. Merek has simply succeeded at his own objective as well.

If they uncover his deception, he may be arrested, killed, or forced to flee.

If he realizes that he has been discovered before they can act, he may escape and remain a threat elsewhere.

All three outcomes are valid.

## Back to Hearthglen

The mission ends when the player characters have brought the survivors back to Hearthglen or otherwise reached a point where they can safely report what happened.

The surviving civilians are taken inside the town and examined for signs of plague, corruption, or other obvious danger before being allowed to remain. Their belongings are searched, their names recorded, and each is questioned about where they have travelled and what they encountered along the way.

Most of this is routine. Refugees have passed through Hearthglen before, and nothing about the group immediately marks them as unusual.

### The Survivors

The seven innocent survivors are treated according to their condition.

Elise's cough receives particular attention, but unless the Gamemaster decides otherwise it proves to be the result of exhaustion, cold, and poor living conditions rather than plague. Brendan remains with her.

Those who are able are eventually given food, a place to sleep, and simple work around Hearthglen while longer-term arrangements are made. Some may later become familiar faces in the town.

If any of the survivors died on the road, their deaths are recorded along with the rest of the mission report.

### Merek Calder

What happens to Merek depends on what the player characters discovered and how they chose to act.

If Merek reaches Hearthglen without being exposed, he is processed alongside the other refugees. Nothing immediately distinguishes him from the rest. He has achieved what he wanted: entry into Scarlet-held territory under a credible identity.

In the days that follow, he begins quietly observing patrol routines, defensive positions, supply movements, and the habits of those stationed in Hearthglen.

If the player characters accuse Merek or bring him back under guard, their officers hear the evidence they have gathered. Suspicion alone may not be enough to establish his guilt, but signs of secret meetings, unexplained knowledge of Scourge movements, contradictions in his story, or testimony from the other survivors may strengthen their case.

If Merek escaped before reaching Hearthglen, his fate remains unknown. The survivors can confirm that he travelled with them and helped keep them alive, but little else about his true identity can be established.

### Epilogue

The survivors brought out of the Plaguelands become part of Hearthglen's growing population of soldiers, refugees, laborers, and displaced families.

For the player characters, the assignment ends as it began: as a rescue mission. Whether they also brought an enemy agent into Hearthglen is another matter.

Ghouls are ravenous undead created by the Scourge, driven by hunger and a crude instinct to tear apart the living. Their decayed bodies lurch forward with brutal determination, attacking with claws and teeth and showing little concern for injury or pain.

### Diseased Wildlife

<br/>Diseased wildlife are common throughout the Eastern and Western Plaguelands. These animals have been exposed to the lingering corruption of the plague and are already beginning to change, though they have not yet become undead.

Any ordinary animal can be turned into a diseased version by adding the Resistance and Fearless properties below. Resistance represents a body that has begun to lose its normal response to pain, injury, and physical trauma, allowing the creature to endure wounds that would quickly drive off or kill a healthy animal. Fearless reflects the same corruption of natural instinct: diseased animals no longer retreat from danger as readily and may continue attacking despite fire, injury, or overwhelming opposition.

These properties make diseased wildlife considerably more dangerous than their healthy counterparts, particularly to poorly equipped characters. As the characters gain access to magical weapons and more powerful

\column
